How it works

The idea

A ballot is decisive when the final margin lands within one vote of a tie. The chance of that is the height of the margin's probability curve at a tie, doubled (one ballot moves the margin by two votes), and divided by the number of votes cast when counting per voter. The curve is centred on the forecast margin and scaled so its win probability matches the forecast. This is the approach of the studies cited below; it is not a forecast of who wins.

Equations and symbol key 
z = μ / σ
P(House race)  = 2 · f(z) / (σ · T)        (per voter)
P(House race)  = 2 · f(z) / σ              (per race)
P(Senate race) = same formula; a state's Senate power adds across its contests
P(combined)    = P(House) + w · P(Senate, voter's state)
Index          = P / (average P)           1.00 = average voter (per voter) or average race (per race)
  • μ expected Democratic-minus-Republican margin, as a fraction of votes cast (positive = Democrat ahead)
  • σ spread (scale) of the final margin. "One per chamber" uses the median of the values implied by each race's win probability; "per race" uses each race's own value where its win probability is between 5–40% or 60–95%, otherwise the chamber median
  • z the forecast margin measured in σ units
  • f height of the margin curve at z: Normal, or Student t with 4 degrees of freedom whose scale is set so its win probability matches the forecast
  • T total votes cast in that race in the 2024 presidential election, used as a turnout proxy
  • P probability that one ballot decides the race
  • w Senate weight (default 4.35 = 435 ÷ 100: a senator is 1/100 of the Senate, a representative 1/435 of the House)
  • Average P per voter: the vote-weighted mean across voters; per race: the simple mean across races
What the choices mean
  • Per voter divides by votes cast, so a voter in a small-turnout race has more power than one in a large-turnout race with the same margin. Per race skips that division and ranks how decisive each race is, regardless of size.
  • The Senate weight only affects the Combined view. A weight of 1 counts a Senate race the same as a House race.
  • States without a Senate race in 2026 have no Senate component.
  • Rankings that change a lot between settings are less reliable than ones that stay put; the search box shows each district's rank range across settings.
Sources

Limits
  • The forecast is another group's work; this page only converts its numbers. Forecasts change, and so will these rankings.
  • 2024 presidential turnout is a stand-in for 2026 turnout, which will be lower and will differ by place.
  • Races with no major-party opponent, or where two candidates of one party advance, show extreme margins and rank at the bottom.
  • The index measures the chance of casting a deciding ballot, not a voter's influence on policy.
